# Handling Unfinished Artifacts 

Created by Meg Palmer 

<img src="objects/zine-radio-chronological.jpg" width="600" alt="a half finished accordion book about Hamm radio">

Part of the ethos of the Expressive Paper workshop series was to slow down. To focus on the process of making, and not just the things made. So why then, were we surprised that what came out of these workshops was often left unfinished? What were we do to with the artifacts from our workshops that weren't—and maybe never would be—fully realized? 

I turn here to three different approaches to the preserving of unfinished works, alongside their implications for digital humanities critical making projects. 

## William Morris: Unfinished Works 

Unfinished objects occupy a unique place in the archives. They are records of what was made, what was not made, and what might have been made. They hold an array of many possible futures. A half finished sketch, a novel never finished, great works . Sometimes, these unfinished works stay in the archive. But sometimes, through creative partnerships, they can live lives beyond the archive as well. 

Of the collection—available for retail through the company William Morris himself founded, Claire Vallis, design director of Sanderson Design Group, has this to say: “The archive has a variety of archival pieces, some sketches, and some more finished artworks intended for different purposes. Borders for carpets have been translated into finished, repeated fabric and wallpaper, including stripes, and it has been a thrill to see these come to life in new iterations,”
